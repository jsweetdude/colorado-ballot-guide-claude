# Decisions

Every choice made in place of asking, with the reason. The brief said not to ask questions; these are the calls that were made instead. Newest at the bottom of each section.

## Process

1. **Worked directly in the repo's single checkout, not a separate git worktree.** The repository was brand new and empty, with no other worktrees and nothing in it but the handoff folder, so no other process could be sharing its `HEAD`.
2. **`brief/` is git-ignored.** It holds handoff material, including notes from John's personal vault about the design system. The design system's stylesheet is copied into `src/styles/porcelain.css` with its source path and copy date, which is what the build needs.
3. **Research ran on Claude Sonnet subagents, not Codex.** John's usual routing sends fetch-and-summarize fan-outs of three or more to Codex. This repo is named `-claude` and the brief forbids reading any other implementation, which points to a side-by-side comparison; sending the research to another vendor's model would mix the two entries. Eleven Sonnet agents worked from one written protocol (`scripts/research-protocol.md`).
4. **Codex cross-model review was skipped** for the same reason.
5. **The repository stays private.** The brief asks for an error-report link to the repo's issues. Making the repository public is an outward-facing change that is John's call, so the link points at the issues page and works for collaborators now and for everyone once the repo is public.

## Data and research

6. **The candidate roster is the Secretary of State's certified list (September 4, 2026), not the handoff inventory.** The inventory was secondary-sourced and differs: the Senate field is Chew (Forward), Baum (Approval Voting), Huber (Libertarian) and Withrow (Unity), with Willoughby only a write-in; CD5 adds Mitchell (American Constitution) and Elworth (Libertarian); CD6 adds McMahon (Libertarian), Ryan (Unity) and Witta (unaffiliated); CD1 adds Milton (Unity) and Humphrey (Libertarian); CD2 to CD4 each add a Libertarian; CD3 adds Brown (unaffiliated); the State Board of Education's third seat is District 7 (Esser, Barton, Klopf); regent races in CD2, CD6 and CD7 each have Republican and Libertarian candidates. Certified write-ins are left out.
7. **Candidates are listed in official ballot order** (major parties, then minor parties, then unaffiliated, each tier by the Secretary of State's July 28 lot drawing; governor tickets by last name). It is the order on the voter's ballot and the most neutral one available. Results tables re-sort by match.
8. **Data is JSON**, one file per candidate and per measure, validated by Zod schemas (`scripts/schema.mjs`) in `npm run build`'s prebuild step. JSON parses strictly and is what the research agents and scripts write natively.
9. **No party-platform inference.** The brief allows party-platform inference as a low-quality source. It was not used: a position needs the candidate's own vote, bill, questionnaire, statement, or a rating about that candidate. Party is visible on every result anyway, and inferring from it would make every minor-party candidate look fully known.
10. **Inferred positions and interest-group ratings are capped at agree or disagree (2 or 4)**, never "strongly", and the schema enforces it. They count at 0.3 and 0.6 quality toward confidence.
11. **Every position carries a short verbatim quote** from its source, and `scripts/verify-sources.mjs` re-fetches each source and checks the quote is on the page. Profiles say when a quote was confirmed this way.
12. **Measure thresholds follow the Blue Book, not the rule of thumb.** The brief says constitutional amendments need 55 percent; the Blue Book says Amendment 87 passes with a simple majority. Each measure shows the threshold the Blue Book states.
13. **Federal campaign finance comes from the FEC API** in one script for all federal candidates, so the numbers share a date and method. State candidates' totals are filled only where a fetched news story or TRACER page reports them.
14. **Election dates are sourced to the Larimer County Clerk's 2026 election page** (October 26 last day to get a ballot by mail and recommended last day to mail one back; ballots received by 7 p.m. November 3), confirmed by fetching it.
15. **State Senate seats on the 2026 ballot** come from the Secretary of State's ballot order page (21 districts). The results page says whether the visitor's Senate district has a race this year.

## Interview and matching

16. **25 statements**, the top of the 20 to 25 range, because 14 measures each need at least one tied statement and the brief's topic list needs the rest. Every measure has a tied statement, so every measure can show a lean. Two statements (Proposition 133, Amendment 85) will rarely separate candidates but are there so those measures get a lean.
17. **Importance weights: low 0.5, normal 1, high 2.**
18. **A candidate is ranked if known on at least a third of the statements the visitor answered, and at least 2.** The brief says "fewer than a third of positions known"; measuring against what the visitor answered (rather than all 25) keeps the rule meaningful for visitors who skip a lot.
19. **Confidence is an all-must-hold rule.** High: coverage at least 60 percent, source quality at least 0.85, margin at least 10 points, at least 5 shared statements. Medium: coverage at least a third, quality at least 0.6, margin at least 4 points, at least 3 shared statements. Low otherwise. Margin is the distance to the nearest ranked neighbor, so a near-tie lowers confidence for both candidates. A single ranked candidate has no margin, and margin is left out for them.
20. **Measure lean = sum of weight × (answer − 3) × direction** over the tied statements; zero or nothing answered is "unclear."
21. **Yes and no leans share one neutral style** (cobalt-soft info badge). Green-for-yes and red-for-no would read as good and bad.
22. **One statement per screen**, with Back, Skip and Next, a review screen at the end, and the step in the URL hash so the browser's Back button works. Focus moves to each new statement's heading.
23. **"Next" without an answer counts as a skip**, and an explicit "Skip this statement" button also clears any answer. Neither blocks the visitor.
24. **Share links carry answers and the congressional district only**, never the street address or legislative districts.

## Product and design

25. **Astro 7 static site plus one Vercel Node function** (`api/districts.js`). Vercel builds Astro with zero configuration and serves `/api` functions alongside it.
26. **The address goes to the function in a POST body, not the URL**, so it never appears in request logs; the function does not log it and sends `Cache-Control: no-store`. It asks the Census Geocoder for the `Public_AR_Current` benchmark and `Current_Current` vintage, which returns the 120th Congress districts and the 2026 state legislative districts.
27. **Fonts are self-hosted from npm (`@fontsource`) instead of Google Fonts.** The brief's privacy line is that nothing but the address lookup leaves the browser; loading Google Fonts would send every visitor's IP to Google. Instrument Sans and Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono are the Calm voice's faces.
28. **Web Awesome components used: `wa-details` (measure and source disclosures), `wa-dialog` and `wa-copy-button` (share link).** Imported one by one from the npm package and bundled. Native HTML with `pc-*` classes everywhere else, as the Porcelain spec prefers.
29. **Grain on the landing and results pages only**, as the brief says; not on the interview, ballot finder, race, profile, measure or About pages, which are forms or tables.
30. **Kiln only for the October 26 and November 3 date cards.**
31. **Hatching marks unknown positions** on profiles and the coverage meters, always with a "No known position" label or legend.
32. **A "Browse races without the interview" path** so the guide is useful to people who will not take a quiz.

## Made during research and review

33. **When the session's 200-search web budget ran out partway through research, agents found pages by fetching instead:** Google News RSS feeds (titles and outlets only; the outlet's own URL is cited, never the Google redirect) and the news sites' own search pages. Asking to raise the budget was not an option under the brief.
34. **The Colorado Sun issue guides and the Denver Post candidate Q&As (both October 2026) are treated as questionnaires.** The Sun's text summarizing a candidate's answer is quoted verbatim from the page and attributed to the Sun.
35. **A candidate's claim about an opponent is never used as the opponent's position.**
36. **"Undecided" is unknown, not neutral.** Phil Weiser said in June he would listen to both sides before deciding on the hunting amendment; that was removed rather than coded 3.
37. **Undated sources are shown as undated.** Agents had used January 1 placeholders or the date a page was read. Those positions carry `date_basis` (`year`, `month` or `accessed`) and display as "2026 (exact date not given)" or "undated page, read October 14, 2026", never as a specific day.
38. **Quotes may skip words only with a visible " ... " or a [bracketed insert].** Two quotes that had been joined across "he said" were rewritten with an ellipsis. Victor Marx's single "Yes" to a three-part Colorado Sun question is shown with the question text so the quote makes sense under each statement.
39. **A lone ranked candidate is labeled "Only ranked candidate", not "Top match".** With nobody else rankable there is no comparison behind the word "top".
40. **Christy Peterson's listed website is not linked.** It resolves to a hacked page serving gambling spam.
41. **Record items must be votes, bills or official decisions.** A Gabe Evans roundtable with a federal official was removed as an event, and press-release wording ("waste, fraud, and abuse", "championed") was rewritten neutrally.
42. **The quote checker follows embedded graphics** (iframes and the Chalkbeat "side-chain" embeds the Sun uses for its regent and State Board guides), because a reader sees that text on the page. House roll-call quotes are checked against the House Clerk's official XML, since the HTML page loads votes with JavaScript.
43. **Measure text is checked against the Blue Book itself** (`scripts/verify-measures.mjs`), allowing for page headers that split a paragraph in the PDF. Ballot Issue 7A's fiscal table is flattened into "label: value" lines; its numbers match the printed table.
44. **Attorney general, secretary of state and treasurer are matched on office-relevant statements only** (9, 5 and 5 statements; listed on the About page and in `races.json`). Candidates for those narrower jobs rarely speak to tariffs or deportation, so against all 25 statements none could reach the one-third bar and those races would never show a match. The one-third rule, the formula and confidence apply unchanged inside each set. Every other race uses all 25. The interview is the same for everyone.
45. **A candidate whose own record points both ways on a statement is coded 3 (mixed), with both halves named.** Jeff Hurd supports Medicaid work requirements but says he voted to extend the enhanced ACA premium credits; his health-coverage position is 3, quoting the latter and noting the former.
46. **Consistency rule for immigration:** a candidate who supports deporting people convicted of serious crimes but opposes broader deportation is coded 2 (disagree) on "Deporting people who are in the U.S. without legal status should be a top federal priority." Pettersen (first coded 1) and Rutinel (first coded 3) were brought in line with Laubacher.
47. **A build review of every major candidate's positions corrected one reversed code:** Jena Griswold's redistricting position had been coded agree from an article saying she backed emergency mid-decade redistricting, which is the opposite side of Amendment 86. It is now 2. One inference too weak to keep (a border-security quote used for fentanyl sentencing) was removed.
48. **An inference must come from a source that names the statement's policy area.** General principles ("government should stay out of your personal decisions", protecting old-growth forests, spending less on weapons abroad) were not used to infer positions on abortion, public-land ownership or fentanyl sentencing; six such inferred positions on minor-party candidates were removed in review.
49. **"Should more federal land be opened to oil and gas drilling?" is a drilling question, not an ownership one.** Answers to it count toward the clean-energy statement (whose coding rule names limits on new drilling) and are not used for "Federal public lands should stay in federal ownership." Seven public-lands positions built on that question were removed; positions that address selling or swapping land stay.

## Process notes

## Gap fill (2026-10-16)

51. **Worked in the single checkout again.** It was clean on `main`, with no other worktree and no uncommitted changes, and the brief asks for commits straight to `main` after each step.
52. **Priority 1 is read literally as major-party challengers** in CD2, CD3, CD4, CD6, CD7 and CD8 plus both major-party treasurer candidates. Jeff Hurd (CD3) and Brittany Pettersen (CD7) are incumbents one position short of the bar, so they head priority 2, which is sorted by shortfall; in practice they were researched right after priority 1 because each is the other half of a race that ranks nobody.
53. **The threshold in the gap list is the full-interview case:** max(ceil(B ÷ 3), 2) known positions for a race whose statement set has B statements (9 of 25; 2 of 5 for treasurer and secretary of state; 3 of 9 for attorney general). A visitor who skips statements changes the bar, so "short N" is a research target, not a promise.
54. **JavaScript-only campaign sites are read in a browser and say so.** Kelley Dennison's site renders nothing without JavaScript, which is why the first pass found no positions there. Its issue pages were read in Chrome; the positions carry a note that the quote was confirmed in a browser, and `verify-sources.mjs` reports them as not found because it fetches without JavaScript.
55. **A stance published on BallotReady counts as a campaign statement only when BallotReady links it to the campaign and the text is in the campaign's voice.** Tim Bennett's gun stance is used that way, labeled, because his own platform page says nothing on guns.
56. **A 2022 state law that raised fentanyl penalties while funding treatment is a mixed position on Proposition 132.** Brittany Pettersen was a Senate prime sponsor of HB22-1326, which made possession of more than 1 gram a felony and paired penalties with treatment; it did not reach any-amount felonies. Coded 3 with both halves named, by the rule in item 45.
57. **Sponsoring a cut to the flat income tax rate supports an inferred position on the income-tax cap.** Kevin Grantham's SB18-061 (2018, did not pass) names the policy area; it does not mention Proposition 136, so the position is inferred and capped at agree.
58. **Roll-call quotes from the General Assembly's vote pages are the member's line, extended to the next member's line when the schema's 10-character minimum requires it** (Gabe Evans's HB24-1313 vote), with the extension explained in the note.
59. **Vote411 is read by entering a public address.** Its candidate answers appear only after an address lookup, so the Mesa County courthouse address was used to reach the statewide races and CD3, and other public addresses for other districts. The brief named Vote411 as a source, which is taken as permission for the lookup. Positions from it cite the ballot page and say the quote was read in a browser, since the verifier cannot reach them.
60. **Amendment 87 and the October 26 date were checked against the Blue Book and the Secretary of State's 2026 election calendar.** The Blue Book's quick reference and the Amendment 87 analysis both say "Passes with a majority vote" (Amendments 81 to 86 say 55 percent). The calendar lists October 26 as the last day to submit a registration and still receive a ballot in the mail, with later applicants directed to a voter service and polling center. The data and landing page already said both; the landing page card now also says registration continues in person through Election Day.
61. **Fairness rule: a race is ranked only when at least two candidates clear the one-third bar.** With one, the better-documented candidate (usually the incumbent, who has a voting record) would be the "top match" in every such race by default. Those races now show every candidate as "not enough known to compare," with each candidate's known positions and the visitor's own answers side by side, and name no top match. `CONFIDENCE_RULES.minimumRanked` holds the number; the About page explains it; three unit tests cover it. The earlier "Only ranked candidate" label is gone.
62. **The blind-recode file carries the coding guide and the quote, not the code.** `research/codes-for-blind-review.json` lists every position with candidate, statement, coding guide, quote, note, source and date, so a second coder works from the same rule without seeing the first pass's 1 to 5 value.
63. **The theme is pinned to light.** The root element now carries `data-theme="light"` and the color-scheme meta says light only, as the gap-fill brief asks; the repository had been following the system scheme. Porcelain's dark-mode rules stay in the stylesheet but no longer apply. The accessibility tests still run their dark-scheme pass, which now checks the same light page.
64. **Scrollable tables are keyboard-focusable regions.** The new side-by-side table (and the match table) sit in a wrapper with `tabindex="0"` and a region label, which the 360px axe check required.
65. **A page that answers a script with an empty HTTP 202 is unreachable, not a missing quote.** On one verification run Ballotpedia did that for 37 quotes confirmed on October 14 (it served the pages again on the next run). The verifier now treats an empty or 202 response as unreachable and keeps an earlier "found" result in that case, instead of reporting a quote as missing from a page it never saw.
66. **Pushes to `main` deploy production through the Vercel Git integration.** Until now every deploy ran `vercel deploy --prod` by hand. On 2026-10-16 `vercel git connect` reported the repository already connected; a README-only push (`1176a93`) produced a new production deployment within a minute, aliased `colorado-ballot-guide-claude-git-main-…`, which only a Git-triggered build gets. CLI deploys still work and are no longer needed after a push.
66. **The five House races stay unranked until the campaign emails come back** (John, 2026-10-16). Even if later research lifts a challenger over the bar, CD2, CD3, CD4, CD6 and CD7 keep the "not enough known to compare" view until the campaigns have had their chance to answer; the fairness rule is not to be relaxed to get them ranked.
67. **Codex's blind codes live in `research/codes-blind-codex.json`,** copied from the sibling checkout on 2026-10-16 for the comparison session (325 items, ids matching `codes-for-blind-review.json`; 129 of them "unknown"). Nothing in it has been applied yet.

## Blind recode (2026-10-16)

68. **Worked in the single checkout again.** Clean on `main`, no other worktree, and the brief asks for a push to `main` after each deliverable; files were staged by name for every commit.
69. **The second coder's codes were compared, not merged.** Codex coded all 325 positions from `research/codes-for-blind-review.json` (statement, coding guide, quote, note, source type, title and date; no first-pass value, no party). Agreement: 141 of 325 exact (43.4 percent); among the 196 Codex coded, 141 exact (71.9 percent) and 194 within one step (99.0 percent); 129 "unknown" (39.7 percent); one direction reversal. Every disagreement was resolved from the coding guide, the quote, the note and the source type, by the protocol and items 45 to 49, and the whole comparison is `research/blind-recode-comparison.md`. Most of Codex's unknowns are roll-call votes and sponsored bills, whose quote is the member's line or the bill title by the protocol's own rule, or answers whose question the note identifies; Codex coded from the quote text alone and did not read the title. Those were kept.
70. **Strength on the 5-point scale follows the scale's own words.** For the within-one-step disagreements: 5 or 1 only where the quote, with its note, states the position without qualification, makes it a signature issue, or is a bill the candidate sponsored that does what the statement says; 4 or 2 where there is a qualification, where only a vote shows it, or where the quote meets only one of the guide's weaker indicators. On that rule 18 codes were taken from Codex (for example Pettersen's clean-energy answer 5 to 4, Barton's "I oppose NN and 87" 2 to 1, Boebert's "never ok" on surgery for minors 4 to 5) and 36 first-pass codes were kept.
